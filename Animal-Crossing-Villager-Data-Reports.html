How to use this page

Six steps, in plain words. You can’t break anything, and nothing you type or open here is saved.

  1. Read the checks first

    A tick means that part of the data looks right. An ! means look closer before you trust a number.

  2. Narrow it down

    Pick a species, personality, hobby, month or gender at the top of the dashboard. Every chart below changes to match.

  3. Tap a bar

    Tap a bar in the species, personality, hobby or birthday charts and the whole page filters to it. Tap it again, or press Clear filters, to go back.

  4. See the exact numbers

    Hover over or tap any bar for its number. Every chart also has Show as a table underneath.

  5. Have some fun

    Look up your birthday twin, press Shuffle to meet a villager, or search the full table at the bottom.

  6. Try your own data

    Save a spreadsheet as a CSV file and open it in Explore your own data. It stays on your device.

    Explore your own data

    Open a spreadsheet saved as CSV, and this page checks it and charts it the same way. Your file never leaves your computer. It isn’t uploaded, stored or saved, by me or anyone. Reload the page and it’s gone.

    1. Open your file. Choose it, drop it in the box, or paste the cells.
    2. Read the checks. They point out blanks, copies and odd values.
    3. Make a chart. Pick what to group by (like Region), then what to measure (a count of rows, or the sum or average of a number column).
    4. Keep it. Press Download as CSV to save the chart’s numbers, or Forget this data when you’re done.
    How do I save my spreadsheet as a CSV?
    • Excel: File → Save As → choose CSV UTF-8 (Comma delimited).
    • Google Sheets: File → Download → Comma-separated values (.csv).
    • Numbers on a Mac: File → Export To → CSV.
    • No file handy? Copy the cells in your spreadsheet, header row included, and use “Or paste rows from a spreadsheet” below.

    The first row should be the column names. One table per file works best.
